A unit that acquires FPV capability without building a training pipeline does not have FPV capability. It has a capability that will degrade to zero within six months and require a contractor to come back and re-establish it.

FFF-411 is built around a different outcome: a unit that finishes the course can operate, sustain, and replicate its FPV capability organically without ongoing contractor involvement. Here is what that looks like in practice.

## What the 10-Day Pipeline Actually Covers

The course runs 10 days. Students complete it with a flight-ready aircraft they built, programmed, and flew themselves. The FAA Part 107 exam is administered during the course. Operators leave with both the certification and the practical skills to employ the platform in the employment domain they trained for.

Day 1 through 4 cover the build. Students receive an unbuilt kit and work through component selection, soldering, frame assembly, and motor installation. The build process is not a facilitation exercise — students make configuration decisions and understand why those decisions affect flight characteristics, reliability, and repairability. An operator who built the aircraft knows how to fix it.

Day 5 covers electronics integration and firmware. Betaflight configuration, motor direction, failsafe settings, and telemetry setup. Students configure their own aircraft and understand what each parameter does. They leave knowing how to reconfigure a platform in the field without calling a contractor.

Day 6 and 7 cover ground school: airspace, regulations, tactical employment doctrine, and mission planning. Part 107 material is integrated here and the exam is administered on Day 7.

Day 8 through 10 are flight operations. Students fly their own aircraft across a progression of tasks — hover, translation, precision maneuvering, and simulated employment profiles. Instructors provide feedback on aircraft control, situational awareness, and employment discipline.

## What the Unit Has at the End

For a four-student cohort completing FFF-411, the unit leaves with:

Four operators holding FAA Part 107 certification and practical employment experience. Four flight-ready aircraft the operators built and understand. The ability to diagnose and repair the most common failure modes without outside support. The curriculum knowledge to train additional operators internally using the platforms as training aids.

What the unit does not leave with: a dependence on a contractor to maintain their program. An operator who built the aircraft, configured the firmware, and flew it for 10 days can troubleshoot a failure. An operator who received a contractor-configured aircraft and received a briefing cannot.

## The Sustainment Calculation

FFF-411 is a 10-day course. The sustainment return from that investment is realized over the operational life of the program — every repair performed by an organic operator is a contractor callout that does not happen, every additional operator trained internally is a training cost that does not recur, and every mission executed without contractor coordination is a readiness cycle that did not require external scheduling.

Units that treat FFF-411 as a one-time event and do not plan for internal replication of the training will rebuild their contractor dependency within 12 to 18 months as attrition removes trained operators. Units that use FFF-402 to certify an internal instructor immediately after FFF-411 do not have that problem.

The pipeline is FFF-411 for operator qualification. [FFF-402](/courses/fff-402/) for instructor certification. That is the complete organic capability package.
