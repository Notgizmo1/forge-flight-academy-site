## What This Guide Covers

This five-chapter guide walks new UAS operators through everything required to fly legally, safely, and effectively in the United States. It is written for operators with no prior aviation background and for experienced pilots transitioning into unmanned systems.

### Chapter 1: Platform Selection

Choosing the right platform starts with understanding the mission requirement. Weight class determines which FAA regulations apply, which airspace you can access, and what level of training is required. A Group 1 platform under 0.55 lbs requires no FAA registration. A platform above 0.55 lbs requires registration and Part 107 certification for commercial or government use. The chapters cover how to match platform capability to mission requirements across surveillance, delivery, inspection, and training applications.

### Chapter 2: FAA Regulations and Part 107

Part 107 is the regulatory framework governing small unmanned aircraft operations in the United States. It covers pilot certification requirements, airspace authorization, operating limitations including visual line of sight, daylight hours, maximum altitude of 400 feet AGL, and waiver procedures for operations that require exceptions. This chapter walks through the certification process, what the knowledge test covers, and how to maintain currency after earning the certificate.

### Chapter 3: Equipment and Configuration

A UAS system is more than the aircraft. Ground control stations, payload configurations, communication links, batteries, and pre-flight inspection procedures all determine whether a mission executes safely and successfully. This chapter covers the equipment stack for common mission types, battery management best practices, pre-flight checklist development, and configuration documentation that supports repeatable, safe operations.

### Chapter 4: Flight Operations

Safe flight operations require understanding airspace structure, weather minimums, emergency procedures, and crew resource management. This chapter covers how to read airspace charts, use FAA tools for airspace authorization, identify and avoid common accident causes, and execute emergency procedures including lost link, battery failure, and flyaway scenarios.

### Chapter 5: Next Steps and Professional Training

The chapters in this guide provide the foundation for legal, safe unmanned aircraft operations. Professional applications in defense, public safety, and research require substantially more capability than the foundation covers. Tactical employment, electronic warfare resistance, autonomous mission execution, field sustainment, and system fabrication are all domains where formal instruction closes the gap between basic competency and operational effectiveness.

All courses are available to DoD units, government agencies, law enforcement, and qualifying commercial organizations.
